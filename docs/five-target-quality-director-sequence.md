# Five-Target Quality Director Outreach and Qualification Guide

## Purpose and contact discipline

The objective is to secure **one qualified discovery call**, not to automate high-volume outreach. Contact five carefully selected Quality Directors at manufacturers where a single part family can be followed from released revision through inspection and named human acceptance. Send each message manually, use one relevant public fact, and stop promptly if the recipient declines, asks not to be contacted, or has no practical fit.

> The honest invitation is a comparison of their current evidence-and-acceptance loop against a narrowly scoped pilot—not a claim that Earthward has already solved their process.

Use only one sentence of personalization. It should be factual and non-invasive, such as: “I noticed that [Company] describes its work in [publicly stated process/product area]; clear evidence around inspection, deviations, and release seems relevant in that setting.” Do not reference personal details, imply knowledge of private audits, or assert that the company has a quality gap.

## Email sequence

The timing is a guide rather than a rule. Stop the sequence as soon as the prospect responds. Do not contact a person more than four times for this pilot without a reply.

## Discovery-call qualification questions

Ask these as an operational conversation, not as a scripted interrogation. Start with their process and only discuss price after the workflow, ownership, and scope are clear.

| Area | Qualification question | Strong fit signal | Concern or disqualifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part-family scope | “Which single part family or product line would be small enough to pilot, but important enough to make the output meaningful?” | One facility and a bounded set of part numbers/revisions can be named. | They need every product, facility, supplier, and workflow included immediately. |
| Evidence retrieval | “When someone asks why a part was accepted or reworked, where do the inspection result, deviation, and approval records live today?” | Multiple systems or handoffs make the current record hard to reconstruct. | Records are already retrieved easily and consistently, with no material friction. |
| Quality decision point | “What has to be true before the person responsible can accept or release the part?” | Clear inspection, evidence, and approval conditions are known. | No accountable human owns release authority. |
| Human ownership | “Who would release the revision, run inspection, open an NCR, recommend disposition, and record final acceptance?” | Named roles can be mapped and a Quality Director can sponsor the policy. | The customer expects automated approval or cannot identify decision owners. |
| Current gaps | “Where are missing reports, unclear revision links, or approval handoffs most likely to appear?” | The team can identify a bounded, repeatable evidence gap. | The request is primarily for generalized analytics or an unrelated automation project. |
| Data availability | “Can you provide a sanitized sample containing a part revision, part-unit identifiers, a few events, and evidence references?” | They can prepare a controlled sample after approval. | They require a production integration before they can evaluate any workflow. |
| Pilot result | “At the end of the pilot, what would make you say the exported record was useful?” | They define a tangible review or retrieval outcome. | Success is defined only as broad regulatory certification or total QMS replacement. |
| Security/operations | “Who needs to review hosted access, retention, attachments, and the escalation path before a pilot can begin?” | A practical review owner and process exist. | An undefined, lengthy procurement process makes the near-term pilot impossible. |
| Commercial authority | “Who would sponsor a fixed $15,000 pilot if the scope and success measure are agreed?” | Director of Quality can sponsor or introduces the economic buyer. | No path to a funded decision exists. |
| Timing | “Is there a current review, customer request, new program, or quality initiative that makes the next 60–90 days a useful window?” | A concrete decision window exists. | No priority or time horizon. |

## Scorecard and next step

Score each category **0, 1, or 2** immediately after the call. A score of 2 means the condition is concrete and confirmed; 1 means plausible but unresolved; 0 means absent or incompatible.

| Category | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bounded part family and facility | Broad enterprise request | Candidate exists | One facility and part family named |
| Evidence-retrieval pain | No meaningful friction | Some friction | Clear recurring reconstruction burden |
| Named human approval authority | Unknown | Role known | Sponsor and approval roles named |
| Sanitized data feasibility | Cannot share | Approval uncertain | Sample package can be prepared |
| Operational buyer | No path | Indirect path | Sponsor/economic path identified |
| $15,000 pilot viability | No budget | Price needs validation | Fixed pilot is plausible within process |
| Timing | No horizon | Future possibility | Active 60–90 day decision window |

| Score | Decision |
|---:|---|
| **11–14** | Advance to a 45-minute workflow-mapping session and send the pilot scope sheet. |
| **8–10** | Keep warm; send a concise recap with the open conditions and schedule one follow-up only if the prospect agrees. |
| **0–7** | Politely disqualify or nurture. Do not expand the product scope to force a fit. |
