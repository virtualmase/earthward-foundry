# Production Commercialization Research Notes

## Evidence base

NIST’s 2024 Supply Chain Traceability Manufacturing Meta-Framework describes trusted data repositories, traceability chains, data integrity, secure access, and traceability event recording as a structured response to industry and regulatory needs. Source: [NIST IR 8536 announcement](https://csrc.nist.gov/News/2024/supply-chain-traceability-manufacturing-framework).

NIST’s smart-manufacturing digital-thread work frames connected design, manufacturing, and inspection information as a way to reduce cycle time, improve first-pass correctness, and support small-to-medium manufacturers through open standards and richer lifecycle data. Source: [Digital Thread for Smart Manufacturing](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/digital-thread-smart-manufacturing).

NIST defines metrological traceability as a documented, unbroken chain of calibrations to specified reference standards, and states that the provider of a measurement result is responsible for supporting its traceability claim. Source: [Metrological Traceability: Frequently Asked Questions and NIST Policy](https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability).

## Implication for Earthward Foundry

Earthward’s credible initial value is not general-purpose autonomous agents. Its existing append-only record, named human approvals, explicit sequence controls, gap reporting, and quality logic align most strongly with a narrow **traceability and quality-decision record** product for a single manufacturing workflow. Any market claim must avoid implying that Earthward itself provides certification, regulatory compliance, or NIST traceability; it should support customer documentation, evidence, and decision controls.
